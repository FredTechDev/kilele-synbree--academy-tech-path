# 1. PROGRAMME OVERVIEW

Kilele TechPath is a structured technology education pathway designed to introduce learners to the world of computing, digital innovation, software development, engineering and emerging technologies.

The programme provides learners with a progressive foundation in technology from Grade 6 through Grade 11.

Rather than introducing programming as the only entry point into technology, Kilele TechPath exposes learners to the broader technology ecosystem.

Learners gradually develop:

- Digital literacy.
- Computer literacy.
- Logical and computational thinking.
- Problem-solving abilities.
- Programming knowledge.
- Data literacy.
- Digital creativity.
- Hardware and networking awareness.
- Artificial intelligence literacy.
- Cybersecurity awareness.
- Collaboration and communication skills.
- Innovation and project development skills.

The programme begins with general technology awareness and gradually introduces practical creation, experimentation and specialization.

By Grade 11, learners should have experienced different technology disciplines, identified areas of interest and developed a portfolio of practical work.

## 1.1 The central learning progression

The programme follows six progressive stages:

| Grade | Stage | Central theme |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 6 | Awareness | Understanding technology |
| Grade 7 | Competence | Using technology productively |
| Grade 8 | Creation | Building simple digital solutions |
| Grade 9 | Exploration | Discovering technology disciplines |
| Grade 10 | Discovery | Exploring specialization pathways |
| Grade 11 | Direction | Developing focused practical expertise |

## 1.2 Programme philosophy

Technology education should not be limited to teaching learners how to use applications or memorize programming commands.

Learners should understand how technology works, why it exists, how it affects society and how they can use it to solve problems.

The programme therefore emphasizes understanding before complexity, experimentation before specialization and practical application alongside theory.

# 2. VISION, MISSION AND PHILOSOPHY

## 2.1 Vision

To develop digitally capable, innovative and technologically confident learners who can understand, create and responsibly use technology to solve real-world problems.

## 2.2 Mission

To provide structured, practical and accessible technology education that prepares learners for further learning and participation in an increasingly digital world.

## 2.3 Core philosophy

Every learner should have an opportunity to discover technology before being expected to specialize in it.

The programme recognizes that learners possess different interests, abilities and learning preferences.

Some learners may enjoy programming. Others may demonstrate stronger interests in design, data, robotics, networking, cybersecurity or digital media.

The programme must provide meaningful exposure to these different disciplines.

## 2.4 Guiding principles

1. Learning by doing.
2. Understanding before memorization.
3. Problem-solving before tool dependency.
4. Creativity alongside technical knowledge.
5. Responsible and ethical technology use.
6. Collaboration and communication.
7. Progressive learning complexity.
8. Practical evidence of competence.
9. Equal opportunity for technology discovery.
10. Continuous exploration and improvement.

# 4. CORE LEARNING PRINCIPLES

## 4.1 Progressive complexity

Every grade should introduce new competencies while reinforcing earlier learning.

For example:

- Grade 6 introduces files and folders.
- Grade 7 introduces organized digital documents.
- Grade 8 introduces programming files and project structures.
- Grade 9 introduces source code repositories.
- Grade 10 introduces collaborative development.
- Grade 11 introduces professional project organization.

## 4.2 Practical learning

Every major topic should include at least one practical activity.

A learner should not only hear about programming. They should write and execute simple programs.

A learner should not only hear about networking. They should identify networking devices and understand how they connect.

A learner should not only hear about cybersecurity. They should practice identifying suspicious messages and protecting digital accounts.

## 4.3 Project-based learning

Learners should complete projects that demonstrate the knowledge acquired.

Projects should gradually increase in complexity and encourage independent thinking.

## 4.4 Exploration before specialization

Grades 6–9 should establish broad technology awareness.

Grades 10–11 should provide deeper exposure to selected pathways.

Specialization must remain flexible and learner-directed.

## 4.5 Real-world relevance

Examples should reflect learners' environments.

Possible contexts include:

- School management.
- Agriculture.
- Healthcare.
- Transportation.
- Environmental conservation.
- Small businesses.
- Financial literacy.
- Community communication.
- Education.
- Public services.

# 16. ASSESSMENT AND EVALUATION FRAMEWORK

Assessment should measure knowledge, practical competence, creativity and problem-solving.

It should not rely entirely on written examinations.

## 16.1 Assessment categories

| Category | Suggested weighting |
|---|---:|
| Conceptual understanding | 20% |
| Practical exercises | 25% |
| Projects | 25% |
| Problem-solving challenges | 15% |
| Documentation and presentation | 10% |
| Reflection and participation | 5% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

These are proposed programme weightings and may be adapted to the applicable school assessment requirements.

## 16.2 Assessment methods

- Short quizzes.
- Practical demonstrations.
- Individual assignments.
- Group projects.
- Oral explanations.
- Digital portfolios.
- Research tasks.
- Project presentations.
- Peer reviews.
- Self-reflection.

## 16.3 Practical project rubric

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---:|
| Understanding of the problem | 15 |
| Planning and design | 15 |
| Technical implementation | 25 |
| Creativity and innovation | 10 |
| Testing and improvement | 15 |
| Documentation | 10 |
| Presentation | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

## 16.4 Competency levels

Use the following developmental scale:

**Level 1 — Emerging**

The learner recognizes basic concepts but requires significant guidance.

**Level 2 — Developing**

The learner performs basic tasks with some assistance.

**Level 3 — Competent**

The learner independently applies concepts to familiar problems.

**Level 4 — Proficient**

The learner adapts knowledge to unfamiliar challenges and demonstrates strong practical understanding.

**Level 5 — Advanced**

The learner independently develops, evaluates and improves solutions and can explain their decisions.

These levels describe learning progress, not fixed intelligence or ability.

4. Examine practical project outcomes.
5. Review the relevance of tools.
6. Update learning materials.
7. Test revised activities.

## 24.3 Version control

Curriculum changes should be documented using version control.

Recommended versioning:

- Major version: Significant curriculum restructuring.
- Minor version: New modules or substantial improvements.
- Patch version: Corrections and small updates.

Example:

- `v1.0.0` — Initial curriculum release.
- `v1.1.0` — Additional learning modules.
- `v1.1.1` — Corrections and clarifications.
- `v2.0.0` — Major curriculum revision.

# 25. CONCLUSION

Kilele TechPath is designed to provide learners with more than basic computer knowledge.

It is a progressive journey into understanding, exploring and creating technology.

From the first experience of operating a computer in Grade 6 to developing a focused technology project in Grade 11, learners should progressively gain the knowledge, skills and confidence needed to participate meaningfully in the digital world.

The programme does not assume that every learner will become a programmer.

Instead, it gives every learner the opportunity to discover their interests, explore different disciplines, develop practical abilities and make informed decisions about future learning.

**The goal is not simply to teach learners how to use technology. It is to help them understand it, question it, create with it and use it responsibly.**
