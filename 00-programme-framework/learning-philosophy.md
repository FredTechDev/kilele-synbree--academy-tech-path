# Learning Philosophy

*Kilele TechPath — Programme Framework*

Technology education should not be limited to teaching learners how to use applications or memorize programming commands.

Learners should understand **how technology works, why it exists, how it affects society and how they can use it to solve problems**.

The programme therefore emphasizes:

- Understanding before complexity.
- Experimentation before specialization.
- Practical application alongside theory.

## The central learning progression

| Grade | Stage | Central theme |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 6 | Awareness | Understanding technology |
| Grade 7 | Competence | Using technology productively |
| Grade 8 | Creation | Building simple digital solutions |
| Grade 9 | Exploration | Discovering technology disciplines |
| Grade 10 | Discovery | Exploring specialization pathways |
| Grade 11 | Direction | Developing focused practical expertise |

## Core learning principles

### Progressive complexity

Every grade introduces new competencies while reinforcing earlier learning.

- Grade 6 introduces files and folders.
- Grade 7 introduces organized digital documents.
- Grade 8 introduces programming files and project structures.
- Grade 9 introduces source code repositories.
- Grade 10 introduces collaborative development.
- Grade 11 introduces professional project organization.

### Practical learning

Every major topic should include at least one practical activity.

- Learners should **write and execute** simple programs, not only hear about programming.
- Learners should **identify networking devices** and understand how they connect.
- Learners should **practice identifying suspicious messages** and protecting accounts.

### Project-based learning

Learners complete projects that demonstrate acquired knowledge. Projects increase gradually in complexity and encourage independent thinking.

### Exploration before specialization

- Grades 6–9 establish broad technology awareness.
- Grades 10–11 provide deeper exposure to selected pathways.
- Specialization remains flexible and learner-directed.

### Real-world relevance

Examples should reflect learners' environments, including:

- School management, agriculture, healthcare, transportation.
- Environmental conservation, small businesses, financial literacy.
- Community communication, education, public services.
